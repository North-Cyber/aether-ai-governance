# Aether 2.0 project brief

## Purpose

**AETHER — AI Governance & Product Risk Intelligence** is an independent learning and portfolio project. It helps its owner practice evaluating how AI products could affect people, what evidence is needed, which safeguards may reduce risk, and what decision should be communicated before release.

AETHER is not a business, consulting service, real-product certification, or claim that any AI system is safe.

## Owner context

The owner is a cybersecurity student at Bellingham Technical College in Washington, preparing for a planned transfer to Western Washington University's Cybersecurity B.S. program in Fall 2027. Long-term interests include technical AI governance, AI product risk, responsible AI, product assurance, and responsible innovation. The preferred work is judgment-oriented and collaborative with product, engineering, security, and leadership teams.

## Practice method

1. Understand intended use, users, data, product boundaries, and context.
2. Identify possible harms and how different people may be affected.
3. Separate observed evidence from assumptions and unknowns.
4. Recommend practical safeguards and identify accountable roles.
5. Make a proportionate release recommendation with conditions and reasons.
6. Communicate the decision and preserve the evidence needed to revisit it.

This is a learning process, not a formal audit method. Where useful, the project may use established risk-management references as organizing aids, while avoiding unsupported compliance claims.

## Design direction

- Calm, intelligent, innovative, trustworthy, and human.
- Subtle Pacific Northwest influence rather than an outdoor tourism theme.
- Warm off-white, muted evergreen, sage, slate, and soft blue.
- Minimalist editorial typography, generous spacing, a simple original geometric mark, and restrained motion.

## Content rules

- Clearly label fictional products and scenarios.
- Never invent test outputs, dates, model versions, architecture, vendor terms, measured outcomes, or launch approvals.
- Treat unavailable evidence as unknown; do not convert unknown into “pass” or “fail.”
- Use proposed roles as owners only when labeled proposed; do not imply real assignments.
- Explain scope and limitations beside the related claim, not only in a footer.
- Use synthetic data only. Never include real patient, employee, or identity information.

## Sharing and publishing
