## Project purpose

AETHER means **AI Governance & Product Risk Intelligence**. It is a personal, independent learning and portfolio project by a cybersecurity student at Bellingham Technical College who plans to transfer to Western Washington University's Cybersecurity B.S. program in Fall 2027. The project practices evaluating AI products for safety, privacy, security, reliability, and responsible use before release.

The owner wants to guide responsible technology decisions alongside product, engineering, security, and leadership teams. This is a learning project—not a company, consulting practice, real-product audit, clinical service, or certification program.

## Current state

- A calm Pacific Northwest visual direction: warm ivory, evergreen, sage, slate, generous spacing, editorial typography, and a simple geometric A mark.
- A static portfolio homepage, three linked case files, and a browser-only AI Evaluation Lab with five fictional scenarios.
- R-01 is an early reliability exercise. Notes report two responses that avoided an exact unverified clinic-hours guess, but the full transcripts and model/version metadata are missing. Do not make broader reliability claims.
- P-01 is a scenario-based healthcare reminder privacy review. The recommendation shifted from initial approval to conditional approval pending privacy safeguards. Do not describe it as a real healthcare product audit.
- E-01 is a fictional tabletop launch review. It includes proposed risk hypotheses, an unrun test plan, unknown likelihoods, launch gates, and a hold recommendation because evidence is missing. Do not invent test results or claim that a real product failed.

## Design and writing rules

- Preserve the calm, human, premium design. Avoid hacker clichés, neon lock graphics, dense dashboards, and tourist-style mountain illustrations.
- Make the work understandable to a recruiter or classmate in a few seconds.
- Keep the site static and portable. No accounts, backend, API key, tracking, or unnecessary dependencies.
- Use plain language, keyboard-accessible controls, readable contrast, responsive layouts, and working links.
- Do not invent real people, organizations, statistics, credentials, employer partnerships, user outcomes, model tests, or contact details.
- Mark every illustrative scenario as fictional and distinguish observed evidence from assumptions, plans, and unknowns.
- Use synthetic data only. Do not add real health information or identifiers.
- Framework references are educational pointers, not claims of formal compliance. Verify current official source pages before changing framework-specific wording.

## Work to complete

1. Audit the supplied source and identify any broken links, unfinished flows, repeated content, unclear claims, mobile issues, accessibility barriers, or inconsistent copies.
2. Improve the homepage and case files only where the changes make the portfolio clearer, more credible, or easier to explore. Preserve source evidence boundaries.
3. Review the evaluation lab's prompt-copying, manual rating, local saving, and report-export flows. Keep the tool explicit that it does not connect to a model or validate AI automatically.
4. Keep E-01's risk register and proposed gates actionable. Likelihood must remain unknown until testing or other evidence supports a rating. The final recommendation should distinguish “evidence is missing” from “the product failed.”
5. Make the project easy for a beginner to maintain. Update the README and handoff notes if file paths or workflows change.
6. Use the local preview to inspect representative desktop and mobile pages and manually follow the main links. Do not deploy or publish.
7. Return the refreshed source as one portable ZIP, plus a concise change summary and the most important remaining evidence gaps.

## Boundaries

Keep the work local and unpublished. Do not create or modify external accounts, connect services, expose credentials, or send messages. Do not add tracking or server-side data collection. Do not claim comprehensive evaluation from the small exercises. Make routine design and implementation choices without asking repeated preference questions; ask only if a genuine blocker requires owner input.

## What “finished” means

A visitor can understand what AETHER is, why it exists, what the student has actually practiced, what remains unknown, and how to open the evaluation lab. Every linked case file is complete enough to review; navigation works; the site remains honest and portable; and the handoff explains exactly how to continue adding evidence and case studies.
