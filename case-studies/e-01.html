Case file 03 · Governance & launch readiness

Keep the release gate evidence-led

A tabletop review of a fictional healthcare assistant. The work maps who could be affected, what evidence is missing, and what should be true before anyone considers a pilot or public launch.

ProductNorthstar CareGuide · fictional
Review typeTabletop risk review
Product testsNot run
Case statusProposed decision gates
Provisional recommendation

Hold the launch decision until the critical evidence gaps are closed. This is an evidence-based pause for a fictional scenario, not a finding that a real product failed. No model outputs, architecture, privacy terms, or operational evidence were supplied or tested.

Scope: Northstar CareGuide and the clinic are invented for this learning exercise. This page is not clinical advice, a real product audit, a legal or regulatory determination, or proof that any AI system is safe. All risk likelihoods remain unknown until evidence is gathered.

Product and intended use

Scenario assumption: Northstar CareGuide is a conversational assistant proposed for a fictional clinic. It would answer routine, clinic-approved service questions and help an adult prepare an appointment request. A staff member would review any generated summary before using it.

The intended boundary excludes diagnosis, treatment recommendations, emergency triage, and autonomous appointment changes. In practice, users may still ask for these things or treat a fluent answer as medical guidance. The design must account for that predictable behavior instead of relying on a disclaimer alone.

PatientsCould get help navigating services, but may rely on an incorrect or overconfident response.
CaregiversMay use a shared device or ask questions for another person, raising privacy and authorization questions.
Clinic staffCould receive clearer requests, but an inaccurate summary could distort what a patient meant.
Product teamsWould need clear ownership for safety, privacy, security, accessibility, vendor, and incident decisions.

Possible benefit, not measured: clearer routine navigation and better-prepared appointment requests. No time savings, accuracy improvement, or user benefit has been measured in this exercise.

Evidence plan before a decision

Keep each result auditable

  • Use a fresh conversation for each run and keep the exact prompt and complete response.
  • Record the provider and model label as shown, date, configuration, and any relevant settings; do not guess missing metadata.
  • Rate the response against criteria set before the test and explain each rating.
  • Repeat meaningful variations; one safe answer is not evidence of broad reliability.
  • Revisit likelihood, residual risk, and launch gates only after the evidence is reviewed.

Proposed launch gates

All seven gates are open: evidence missing. Each needs an accountable owner, reviewable evidence, and a recorded acceptance decision. Roles below are proposed, not assigned. A pilot is not automatically safe just because it is small.

Keep the launch on hold while required evidence or acceptance is missing; reopen affected gates when models, prompts, data use, integrations, intended users, or incident evidence change.

Decision memo

To: Fictional product leadership

From: AETHER tabletop review · Subject: Northstar CareGuide release readiness · Decision: Hold the launch decision pending evidence.

The proposed product could help people navigate routine clinic services, but the scenario does not include evidence needed to judge how it behaves, what data it processes, or how it is operated. The most consequential open questions involve urgent symptom handling, sensitive health data, identity and appointment actions, accessibility, and accountable human oversight.

Recommendation: Do not approve public or unsupervised launch based on this record. Keep work in a synthetic-data evaluation environment while owners document the product boundary and close the gates above. Reconsider a limited pilot only after the responsible clinical, privacy, security, product, and operations owners review evidence against criteria defined in advance.

This is not a claim that a real system failed, and it is not a final verdict on a real product. Missing evidence is the reason to defer the decision; it should not be described as proof of either safety or harm.

Framework note

This review uses a simple sequence to organize the work. It loosely aligns with the NIST AI Risk Management Framework's high-level functions; it does not claim formal conformity, certification, or completion of a NIST assessment.

GovernClarify who is accountable for the decision, the boundaries, and follow-up.
MapDescribe intended use, affected people, context, and possible harms.
MeasureGather repeatable evidence, record uncertainty, and assess risks.
ManagePrioritize safeguards, assign proposed owners, and make a reviewable decision.
