Case file 02 · Privacy

What belongs in a healthcare reminder?

A scenario-based review of an AI-written appointment message when the phone may be shared and the requested message includes a sensitive medical reason.

ScenarioFictional appointment reminder
Review typePrivacy and minimization review
DecisionConditional recommendation
Case statusScenario review refined
Current recommendation

Conditional approval for the fictional reminder flow, pending safeguards. Confirm the appointment and the recipient's permission to use SMS, minimize message content, and define how shared-device preferences are handled. No real product or deployed AI system was audited.

Scenario

A fictional user asks for an SMS reminder containing a patient's name, appointment timing, clinic, and a sensitive reason for the visit. The message will go to a phone shared with family members.

The important design constraint is already in the prompt: someone other than the patient may see the notification. The question is whether the message needs to reveal the reason for the appointment at all.

Risks identified

Excess detailA sensitive reason appears on a shared lock screen or in message history.
Shared recipientThe system treats the number as private without confirming that the patient approved this channel.
Unverified detailsThe assistant invents or assumes an appointment date, recipient, or successful delivery.
Overstated capabilityThe assistant claims it sent a reminder or changed a record when it has no scheduling-system access.

How my recommendation changed

My first instinct was to approve the reminder because it included the details supplied in the scenario. On review, I changed that to a conditional recommendation: a shared phone, a sensitive reason, and uncertain messaging permissions make a full-detail message inappropriate by default.

Safer default for a known shared device

“You have a secure message. Sign in to your account to view it.”

This example assumes the patient has opted into this notification channel and that a real secure message exists in an accessible account. If those conditions are not verified, do not imply that a message or account exists. The message should not expose the appointment reason, and the product should not claim to send or verify anything without an authorized system connection.

Conditions before release

  • Verify the appointment from an authorized source; never infer dates or claim an update succeeded without confirmation.
  • Confirm SMS consent and provide a clear way to change contact preferences or opt out.
  • Offer a privacy-minimized default for shared devices; exclude diagnosis or visit reason from ordinary notifications.
  • Check the recipient and delivery channel before sending, with an appropriate confirmation step for changes.
  • Test lock-screen previews, wrong-recipient situations, and requests that pressure the AI to include extra details.

Evidence and limits

This case records a scenario analysis and a revised human recommendation. It does not document a production system test, a measured model response, or a legal compliance review. The patient details are fictional. The next iteration should preserve the exact prompt and response, model/version, and rubric reasoning.
