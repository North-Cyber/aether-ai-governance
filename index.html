01 / The idea

A practice space for responsible release decisions.

AI products can be useful and still create risks. AETHER is where I practice asking what could go wrong, what the evidence shows, and what a product team could change before launch.

In plain language: I take a fictional AI product scenario, review the available evidence or plan a focused test, and examine the result for privacy, reliability, safety, and accessibility. Then I explain the evidence, name the remaining uncertainty, and recommend a responsible next step.

The decision questionIs this ready for people—or does it need another look?AETHER practices the judgment behind that decision. It does not certify real products.
02 / The method

Move from concern to clear next step.

A useful review connects product context to observed behavior, then makes the recommendation specific enough to act on.

01 / FRAME

Understand the use

Who is affected, what the system does, and what information is involved?

02 / MAP

Identify risk

Where could privacy, safety, accuracy, or access break down?

03 / MEASURE

Evaluate evidence

Test a defined scenario and record what is known and missing.

04 / IMPROVE

Recommend safeguards

Describe practical changes and the concerns they address.

05 / DECIDE

Assess readiness

State whether to proceed, add conditions, pause, or reassess.

06 / COMMUNICATE

Explain the decision

Show the evidence, reasoning, owners, and next steps.

05 / Why I’m building it

I want to help guide what technology becomes.

I’m studying cybersecurity at Bellingham Technical College and plan to transfer to Western Washington University’s Cybersecurity B.S. program in Fall 2027. As I build my technical foundation, I’m especially drawn to the decisions that shape how new products affect people.

AETHER helps me practice that work: understand the system, weigh the evidence, recommend safeguards, and communicate a clear decision. I’m building toward roles in technical AI governance, AI product risk, and responsible innovation.

Honest scope matters

AETHER is an independent learning and portfolio project. Its scenarios are fictional, its results are human judgments of specific responses, and its current exercises are small pilots. They do not establish that a model is safe across other prompts or suitable for clinical use.

Better decisions need clear evidence—and a clear account of what remains unknown.
